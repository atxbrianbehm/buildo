**Direction from the user, 2026-10-30:** The current family is not street-ready: it repeats a narrow building with small proportional changes. The goal is a structural language rich enough to receive metagrinder evidence and generate related buildings with distinct architectural identities. The five supplied reference images are visual direction, not licensed assets, measured surveys, or proof of a particular location, date, style or metagrinder dataset.

**Overnight objective:** Build a functional, artist-friendly procedural building language that can generate a family of visibly distinct, related buildings with real architectural geometry, guided by the supplied references and designed to accept verifiable metagrinder/UTDG evidence. Execute the next unblocked architectural-language packet sequentially, testing and recording each result, updating this plan, and publishing each packet as a separate stacked PR without merging. Continue through independent implementation, integration and review preparation without stopping between packets. Preserve deterministic semantic identities, authored choices and locks, source provenance, existing files and structural/material separation. Use demo choices where real evidence is unavailable and clearly mark them; never invent upstream coverage. Keep the existing Phase F street gate and stop for an actual required human decision or unavailable external access, not for routine implementation choices. Do not treat a source-only contract, a repeated facade or passing tests without rendered product evidence as the finished outcome.

## What the references ask the system to express

The images show a shared commercial-street context with individual facades: different building widths and heights; differently proportioned upper storeys and bays; rectangular, arched and grouped upper openings; variation in head treatments, piers, spandrels and cornices; distinct parapet silhouettes; and storefronts with different entrance arrangements, transoms, signs and awnings. Some buildings repeat an element internally while differing markedly from their neighbors. Images 1 and 3 particularly motivate arched upper openings and grouped windows; images 4 and 5 show that signage and awnings belong to storefront identity, but do not supply reliable dimensions. These are observations at image resolution, not automatic historical assertions.

The current sample locks its footprint and retains the same three storeys, four bays, window, storefront, cornice and parapet choices across six members. Heights vary, but that is not the diversity the references require. Current `constructionGenerationResolver.ts` starts from a pinned recipe graph and primarily resolves numeric parameters; `constructionGeneration.ts` stores source claims and confidence but has no typed architectural-claim-to-choice mapping. Window layouts, frontage, cornice and parapet exist as authored graph vocabulary, but family generation does not independently compose them into visibly different identities. The graph, compiler and semantic material bindings remain the single execution path.

## Design principles

1. **A building has a composition, not a bag of random parts.** Distinguish building mass and roofline, facade zones, bay groups and individual openings; express dependencies such as an opening head fitting its wall, frame and glazing. Repetition is a deliberate within-building rule; variation between buildings is an explicit seeded choice, not a guarantee that arbitrary combinations look right.
2. **Evidence has a scope and a status.** A future metagrinder adapter must map actual upstream records into typed observations with source identifiers, locators, confidence, review status and observed/inferred/generated distinctions. Absence remains unknown; conflicting evidence is visible. First inspect the actual upstream contract and data before inventing a mapping or historical claim. A user choice can override a prior without rewriting its evidence.
3. **Choices must execute.** Every supported architectural term maps to graph operators and actual geometry; unsupported terms return a diagnostic and remain inspectable. The shared material source describes appearance, not the shape of an opening. A structural edit does not regenerate material artifacts.
4. **Artist controls stay legible.** Use grouped choices such as opening head, bay grouping and storefront arrangement, with selected scope and lock behavior visible. Preserve advanced graph access, existing source compatibility and local exceptions. Avoid requiring a node graph for the ordinary task.
5. **Reproducible without sameness.** Use semantic seed domains per building and per choice, stable IDs and explicit constraints. Authored locks win within their stated scopes. Reject infeasible combinations or preserve the last valid scene with a reason. Do not promise every seed is unique or add arbitrary offsets solely to force numerical differences.

## First vertical slice: arched upper openings

The first concrete vocabulary addition is a round-headed upper opening alongside the current rectangular opening. It must change the wall aperture, reveal, frame and glazing together, with a real curved head assembled procedurally and coherent material mapping. It must be selectable at an explicit upper-facade scope and retained across save/open and member editing. Start with a bounded, clearly labelled demo choice; do not infer curvature or dimensions from low-resolution photographs. Segmental arches, elaborate carved surrounds, mixed heads within a bay group and historical attribution remain later work unless explicitly admitted.
