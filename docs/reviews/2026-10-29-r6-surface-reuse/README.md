# R6.P4.2 — family-scoped surface pixel reuse

Family preparation now uses a bounded, temporary cache of pixel results keyed by the atlas content pin, complete manifest hash, tile algorithm, resolved layers and base-appearance hash. It retains no geometry or binding artifact from another member. Each member still executes one full compositor admission, including validation of its owned atlas bytes and its own semantic binding artifact, before any cached pixel work is reused. The cache is limited to 128 entries and 32 MiB and is cleared when preparation finishes or fails. Each member creates and owns its own material and texture resources.

On the same local workstation and three-cycle workflow as the [baseline](../2026-10-29-r6-local-performance/README.md), median family return fell from 2,500 ms to 1,791 ms on WebGPU and from 1,959 ms to 1,269 ms on WebGL. These are wall-clock observations from one high-end workstation, not frame times, a universal speed guarantee or a representative-machine acceptance. The optimized [WebGPU](webgpu-performance.json) and [WebGL](webgl-performance.json) receipts retain the individual samples. The instrumented [WebGPU trace](webgpu-profile.json) shows the final six members evaluating `3, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1` recipes instead of `3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3` in the baseline; each member's first evaluation retains the full validation path.

The [comparison](comparison.json), rebuilt by [compare.py](compare.py), verifies the three saved family files per backend are byte-identical to the baseline and that the actual edited-window and family-view screenshots are pixel-identical to the prior captures on both backends. The [member workflow](verify-member.mjs) also checks sibling/source/material identity, semantic window targeting and no extra procedural-provider calls. The [performance replay](verify-performance.mjs) and [profile replay](verify-profile.mjs) require the locally retained P3.1 family file and Chromium.

The focused surface tests compare all texture-channel hashes to independent composition, require separate resource ownership, vary a member's finish, reject altered atlas bytes and an orphaned local target, and confirm that a changed manifest is not reused. They also check cache disposal. The three related surface/workbench suites passed 55 tests. All five standard checks passed: typecheck, lint, build, 1,965 tests across 138 files, and end-to-end smoke. A further focused texture-channel check passed after the full suite; the build retains its existing large-chunk warning. This does not change material generation, family source format or historical UTDG coverage. It does not establish representative-device performance or persistent cross-session caching.
