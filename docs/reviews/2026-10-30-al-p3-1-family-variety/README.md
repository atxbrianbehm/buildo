# AL.P3.1 — opt-in architectural family variation

The Related buildings panel can now vary opening heads/sash, ground frontage, and cornice/parapet independently. Unchecked groups keep the authored building's choices. An active opening lock conflicts with requested opening or frontage variation instead of being silently ignored; pinned locks on affected paths also reject. Each selected family member displays its actual graph choices. The six-combination catalog is explicitly a demo, and its seeded rotation is a bounded starting point rather than an open-ended historical grammar.

The family source format 0.3 stores the initial composition plan and seed with the original building, six editable member graphs and one shared material source. The plan records the generation choices; subsequent member edits legitimately change member graphs without rewriting that initial plan. Existing family source 0.2 remains readable. Generation evaluates each composed graph and rejects a candidate that loses a local surface target or cannot produce distinct executed geometry. With no selected variation groups, the original family path stays in use.

Both [WebGPU](webgpu/family-after.png) and [WebGL](webgl/family-after.png) ran the actual app from the saved round-headed building, enabled all three groups, generated and saved the family, edited building 2 and one selected window, exercised member undo/redo, saved and reopened it. [WebGPU member](webgpu/member-window.png), [WebGL member](webgl/member-window.png), [WebGPU receipt](webgpu/receipt.json), and [WebGL receipt](webgl/receipt.json) retain the observed choices, unchanged siblings and original source, unchanged material hash, and zero new provider calls. [Workflow snapshot](verify.mjs.txt) requires the locally retained AL.P2 source; [artifact verifier](verify-artifacts.mjs) validates its saved results. A preflight screenshot was captured before the final checkbox spacing correction and is excluded from final visual evidence.

A separate run on each backend edited the head of each of the six members. The [validated WebGPU](webgpu/all-members-receipt.json) and [WebGL](webgl/all-members-receipt.json) receipts confirm each graph changed only as requested, with member lineage, original source, initial composition and shared material retained. A fresh-page explicit reopen and resave of the family containing all six edits produced the identical file on [WebGPU](webgpu/six-reopen-receipt.json) and [WebGL](webgl/six-reopen-receipt.json). [Edit workflow](all-members.mjs.txt), [reopen workflow](reopen-all.mjs.txt) and [artifact verifier](verify-all-members.mjs) use local saved-family files. The first two all-member attempts are excluded: an unscoped browser selector matched both the hidden parent and visible member workspace; the successful retries scope it to the visible workspace.

The six compositions visibly vary opening heads, sash, storefront, cornice and parapet, but remain variations of the same rectangular masonry mass with the same materials. They do not yet match the range of the user's street references. The dark arched surround and thin reddish sill line identified in AL.P2 remain. These images require human visual review and do not pass the Phase F street gate, establish a historical style or demonstrate representative-machine performance.

All five final-source checks passed: typecheck, lint, build, 1,988 tests across 142 files, and E2E smoke. A focused UI test also verifies selection of the three variation groups without modifying the source building. The build retains its large-chunk warning; WebGL reports its existing shadow-filter fallback.
