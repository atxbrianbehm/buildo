# T6 — controlled shadow-bias sweep

Changing shadow bias alone is not established as a safe fix for the fringed cornice. More-negative depth bias visibly brightens the recessed backing and shifts nearby shadows, while T5's physical ray test shows the default key light is geometrically blocked at sampled exposed backing points. Normal-bias changes also alter thousands of pixels and have no demonstrated broad quality win in this one view. No product values change in this packet.

On both actual WebGL and WebGPU, the reviewed textured bracket view was held to an exactly matching camera for all captures. Five temporary settings were tested against the actual default depth bias `-0.00003` and normal bias `0.001`: depth biases `-0.00015`, `-0.0005`, `-0.001`, and normal biases `0.003`, `0.01`. All ten restoration frames match their backend's baseline native pixels exactly. Original geometry, bindings, material channels and354 code/config/test pins remain exact; no material/provider generation occurs. [Verification](verification.json), [image metrics](image-metrics.json), [WebGL views](webgl/), [WebGPU views](webgpu/).

The region `[60,355,850,430]` is the same925×849 close view used in T4/T5. At depth bias `-0.001`,19,704 WebGL and20,749 WebGPU region pixels become more than ten mean sRGB channel values brighter than baseline. This is an image comparison, not radiometry or a guarantee of correct shadowing. The same setting changes65,054 and72,959 full-frame pixels respectively, including contact shadows outside the cornice. Lesser bias and normal-bias results are recorded separately.

To connect the bias sweep with the physical geometry, [projected ray samples](projected-ray-samples.json) start from T5's exposed backing grid, discard points behind bracket widths and points occluded from the camera by cornice triangles, and project the remaining light-occluded points to the nearest native pixel. There are123 samples on each backend. At `-0.001`,52 of123 sampled pixels brighten by more than ten mean sRGB channel values on each backend; `-0.0005` brightens25 and26 respectively. This supports concern about light leakage in a physically occluded region, but the sample does not prove the GPU's winning fragment or every pixel's ownership. [Reproduction script](project-rays.py) uses the archived T5 compiled triangles and P0 plan at the recorded paths.

The sweep is limited to one building, one close camera and the default light. It does not rule out a carefully bounded bias combined with other shadow improvements. Next investigate shadow-map resolution/frustum and filtering at multiple views and light angles while preserving real contact shadows; do not ship a larger bias solely to brighten the form. The C9 visual decision is still pending. Earlier failed bias attempts remain in the separate [T4 evidence](../2026-10-27-t4-shadow-discriminator/README.md).
