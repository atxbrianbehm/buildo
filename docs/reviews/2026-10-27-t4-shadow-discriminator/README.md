# T4 — cornice and sill shadow discriminator

The reviewed bracket course looks unusually dark. Controlled actual WebGL and WebGPU captures show that the cornice mesh is both a caster and a receiver for the shadow affecting its visible face. Temporarily disabling either cornice casting or cornice reception brightens the same close-view area on each backend; disabling wall reception does not alter that area. The cornice is one submitted batch containing backing, coping and repeated pieces, so this experiment does **not** identify a particular casting surface or prove whether the darkness is erroneous self-shadowing, physically intended occlusion, or both.

The same diagnostic was run on a real regular-window/sill close view. Disabling cornice casting changed other pixels but changed zero pixels in the selected sill-region rectangle on either backend. Disabling wall shadow reception affected part of that rectangle. These are camera-specific observations, not a general claim that all sill artifacts are resolved. Texture-normal and map-disabled views remain in the prior [C9 packet](../2026-10-27-c9-p2-cornice-authoring/README.md); T1/T2 geometry repairs remain intact.

For each backend, the actual reviewed textured source and bracket treatment were rendered at fixed camera and light, with four temporary diagnostic conditions on each of two views. Materials were cloned and disposed; original materials and cast/receive flags were restored. All **16 restored native PNGs and camera matrices** exactly match their respective baselines; semantic geometry, material channels and finish bindings remain exact. No provider or material generation occurred during the tests. All354 prior code/config/test pins match the C9 source. [Verification](verification.json), [region measurements](metrics.json), [WebGL captures](webgl/) and [WebGPU captures](webgpu/).

The bracket region is the fixed image rectangle `[60,355,850,430]` in a925×849 native frame. Cornice-cast-off and cornice-receive-off both change45,055 pixels in that region on WebGL and45,033 on WebGPU, with identical mean absolute per-channel differences within each backend; wall-receive-off changes none there. Whole-frame differences also include legitimate shadows cast elsewhere, so turning all cornice shadows off is not offered as a product fix. See [region measurements](metrics.json) for the separate sill rectangle and full-frame counts.

Three attempts are retained as [excluded](excluded/). WebGPU produced a `depthTexture` error when the diagnostic toggled the shadow light off and back on; the passing retry kept the light active and toggled receivers instead. Two WebGL shadow-bias sweeps failed exact frame restoration; the second passed the bracket sweep but failed on the sill. Their candidate settings are not accepted as evidence for a fix. These diagnostic failures did not change production source.

Next, isolate the actual cornice sub-surfaces and light/frustum contribution in a controlled renderer experiment before choosing a repair. Verify preserved contact shadows and both backends at whole-building and close scale; compare the real stone colour/normal/ORM finish and clay, retain source/material/geometry, and inspect camera/light and resource restoration. A new product change requires its own small packet and visual review. The existing C9 treatment decision remains pending; this diagnostic neither accepts the treatments nor changes the original image set.
