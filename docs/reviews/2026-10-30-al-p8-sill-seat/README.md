# AL.P8.0: Sill reveal seat

The fine red strip between a regular recessed window and its projecting sill contains the brick pattern: the aperture's masonry reveal was exposed between the frame and the outer trim. A thin procedural stone seat now spans that reveal from the facade plane to the frame, with its visible top 20 mm above the masonry surface. Flat and round-head windows use the same physical construction, including grouped windows. The aperture, frame, glazing and material slots are unchanged; the prototype key and expected compiled geometry hash change. Standalone legacy bay previews retain their previous geometry.

The [before WebGPU](webgpu-before-03/textured.png) and [after WebGPU](webgpu-after-03/textured.png) use the same saved construction and exactly matching camera matrices; [WebGL before](webgl-before-03/textured.png) and [after](webgl-after-03/textured.png) likewise match within that backend. The before comparison removes only the two seat additions through a temporary Vite transform; it is not a historical checkout. [Clay before](webgpu-before-03/clay.png) and [after](webgpu-after-03/clay.png) show the physical change without the textured presentation. The brick strip is covered in these views; a fine seam at the frame remains, and still images do not prove an absence of flicker under all lighting and camera motion.

Thirty focused tests passed across sill-seat, flat/local opening, round-head and grouped-bay geometry. They check seat dimensions, clear elevation above the reveal, finite geometry, material role, legacy parity and intentional changed geometry. Full repository validation is pending. The capture workflow uses the locally retained C10 project in `test-results/c10-p2-2026-10-29/webgpu-04/raised-v10.json`. No historical attribution or user visual acceptance is inferred.
