# T8 — fit construction shadows to building bounds

The construction viewer fits the key-light shadow camera to the world-space building bounding box with a 10% margin, and refits when the light moves or resets. The existing 4096 map, depth bias, normal bias and near/far planes stay unchanged. Other viewer lighting modes are unchanged. The fit is renderer-only; it does not change geometry, material generation or serialized artifacts.

Four differently proportioned bounds and four light angles are checked for all eight corners inside the fitted projection and existing depth range. A default-light ground-ray check covers the unchanged depth range and fitted projection. These tests do not establish all possible imported geometry or ground shadows at extreme low light; the old depth planes are retained. Empty and nonfinite bounds retain the prior projection.

On both native WebGPU and WebGL, the actual implementation captures five views, including close and sill views and a real Shift-drag light movement. The projection half-extent changes from 12.2 m at default light to 10.3 m after lowering the light, and Reset restores the default. Each run verifies compiled geometry, atlas channels and material bindings across the five views, with no additional provider or material-generation calls. The WebGL baseline and candidate also have equal compiled geometry, atlas channels and material bindings in all five views. See the two run receipts and `verify-native.mjs.txt` for the capture procedure and assertions. This uses the existing approved Chromium workflow and existing local T2 project fixture; the observer instruments rendering but does not alter the product shadow projection.

For three views with exactly matching camera, light, geometry and bindings, [the comparison](actual-reference-comparison.json) measures per-pixel sRGB differences against the T7 8192-map reference. In the sill view the baseline mean error is 5.56 and the candidate is 4.03 on considered pixels; 16,904 pixels are closer with the candidate and 6,626 with the baseline. Whole/default and whole/lower also improve on average. The reference is finite-resolution, not ground truth; two independently interacted close cameras differed and their pixel comparisons are excluded. Hard edges and fringe remain visible, and visual acceptance is separate from this engineering result.
