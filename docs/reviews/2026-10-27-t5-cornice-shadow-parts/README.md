# T5 — isolate the cornice's shadow-casting parts

The dark bracket-course backing is primarily shadowed by the **projecting lip of its continuous backing profile** under the current key light. This is more specific than T4's finding that the whole cornice batch was involved. The coping affects shadows elsewhere but changes zero pixels in the measured bracket region; removing repeated-bracket casting changes six pixels there on WebGL and15 on WebGPU. Suppressing the backing's casting brightens44,838 and43,878 pixels respectively in that same region. [Measurements](metrics.json).

The experiment reconstructs the actual submitted indices into backing, coping and78 bracket units, then uses a temporary shadow-only proxy. The reconstructed full proxy produces an **exact baseline native image** on both backends. Seven caster selections per backend are compared, and all14 original native frames and camera matrices restore exactly afterward. Visible geometry and real material bindings remain in place throughout; each temporary proxy and material is disposed. The captured source, material channels, semantic geometry and354 production code/config/test pins remain exact; no material generation occurs. [WebGL](webgl/receipt.json), [WebGPU](webgpu/receipt.json).

An independent [geometric ray check](geometric-ray-proof.json) uses the actual compiled triangle positions and the existing key-light direction. It samples a41×17 grid on the front backing, excluding170 points behind bracket widths. Of527 remaining points,486 rays first meet the underside of the backing's upper lip and41 first meet a bracket; none are clear. This establishes geometric occlusion for these sampled points and this light, not every pixel or view and not the raster owner's identity. The closest intersection is over5 mm from its ray origin, rather than the starting plane. The profile lip spans physical depth0.0216–0.1296 m at height10.64 m in this reviewed source. [Ray script](geometric-rays.py), [compiled triangles](compiled-cornice.json).

These results distinguish the broad physical shadow from the separate dark, fringed edge quality. Removing all backing shadows brightens the detail but also changes other contact shadows, so it is a diagnostic—not a proposed product fix. A renderer or lighting change should preserve useful shadows, be checked at multiple light angles and both backends, and undergo its own visual review. The pending C9 treatment decision remains open.

The existing **Shift + drag** light control can reveal this detail without disabling shadows: a45-pixel downward drag in the925×849 test view lowers the key light. Both native backends retain geometry and material bindings, and Reset view restores the original camera, light and native pixels exactly. [Default and lower-light WebGPU views](light-webgpu/), [WebGL views](light-webgl/). During the drag, maximum camera-inverse-matrix element differences were0.0001982 on WebGPU and0.0006136 on WebGL, below the explicitly recorded0.001 comparison bound; the intermediate camera is not claimed byte-identical. The lower light also makes the remaining shadow fringe visible, so this is a useful review angle rather than a completed lighting repair.
