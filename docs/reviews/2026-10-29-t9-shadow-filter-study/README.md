# T9 — native shadow-filter study

Buildo requests `PCFSoftShadowMap` on both renderer paths. In the installed Three.js 0.184.0, `WebGLShadowMap.js` changes that request to `PCFShadowMap` during shadow rendering and warns that the former is deprecated. The WebGPU `ShadowNode` uses a separate soft-PCF function. These are facts about the installed version, not a claim about other Three.js versions.

Eight fresh Chromium runs compare the actual product setting with a temporary Vite source transform selecting regular PCF. Each backend has a normal five-view pair and a second pair that fixes the two close cameras to the prior actual WebGPU view, drains orbit damping and restores its setting. The whole and sill cameras use the normal UI. Every run verifies geometry, texture channels, material bindings, source continuity and zero additional provider/material calls. All 356 pinned code, config and test files are identical across runs and at verification. The source transform is experimental; no product filter was changed.

In the [fixed-camera comparison](fixed-comparison.json), all five cameras, lights, compiled geometry, bindings and texture channels match within each backend. WebGL resolves both settings to PCF and all five native images are pixel-identical. In the initial independent pair, the lower-light WebGL image differs by one channel in one pixel; the initial close cameras differ and those pixel comparisons are excluded. WebGPU actually selects soft PCF versus regular PCF, and the matched images differ: 56,333 pixels in the close/default view and 34,380 in the sill view, with full-frame mean absolute channel differences of 0.312 and 0.119 respectively. Both retain visible contact shadows, and the inspected close views retain a coarse fringe; a pixel difference is not a quality ranking. No alternate filter is promoted.

An attempted live WebGPU filter swap is retained under `excluded/`: its first whole view restored exactly, but the close/default restoration changed 53,560 pixels with the same recorded camera. The cause was not established. It is excluded rather than counted as a successful same-camera comparison. The fresh fixed-camera runs avoid that live swap and document their camera intervention.

All captures use the same construction fixture, 4096 map, T8 fitted extents and unchanged bias. They cover this building and two light angles, not every building, shadow edge, machine or runtime. The C9 cornice decision and visual-quality acceptance remain pending. Reproducers are archived as `.mjs.txt` to prevent linting of immutable evidence snapshots; they use the existing local T2 project fixture and C9 pin list described in the script.
