# AL.P11.74: Arched masonry hood study

This isolated procedural study builds separate closed stone wedges over an existing round-headed upper window. The request controls stone count, band width, joint width, projection and keystone rise. The keystone also projects forward by half its rise, capped at 30 mm. The planner checks masonry clearance, keeps the faceted inner edge outside the aperture, and replays its versioned plan. The geometry has closed faces, unit normals and metre-space UV coordinates. No preassembled mesh is used.

The [before](before.png), [after](after.png), [detail](detail.png) and [angled](angled.png) views overlay the generated stones on an actual compiled building. Alternating seven- and nine-stone demo arrangements are explicit study choices, not historical inferences or seeded family variation. The hood uses the existing flat trim material; the brick wall is textured. The narrow dark reveal between hood and recessed frame remains a visual question, and UV behavior with a textured stone material has not been established. Sills are unchanged. This overlay is not part of a saved construction graph, the worker compiler path, an artist control or a family archive.

The [rejected initial view](rejected-winding.png) showed dark shading from mirrored winding. The study renderer now reverses the winding after reflecting the geometry, and the corrected views show the existing trim color. Reproduce with `node docs/reviews/2026-10-31-al-p11-74-arched-hood-study/render.mjs`. Focused tests check deterministic replay, separate joints, closed meshes, normals, UV finiteness, keystone depth, wide-aperture clearance, unsupported counts and invalid source plans. Visual approval and integration remain separate.

Validation passed: typecheck, lint, production build, focused hood tests, full suite with two workers (2,159 tests in 192 files), standard browser smoke, and the WebGL overlay render. Two workers were used because an existing family-edit wait had timed out under default concurrency in earlier runs. The build retains its existing chunk-size warning.
